Steal a starter prompt
Ready-to-use prompts
Write a 4-message follow-up sequence for a lead who got a quote but hasn't replied. Day 1: gentle nudge. Day 3: value add. Day 7: scarcity / soft deadline. Day 14: graceful exit. Match my tone — professional, warm, not pushy.
Here's a client's enquiry and their requirements. Write a personalised proposal for them. Use my standard structure: intro, understanding of their problem, proposed approach, deliverables, pricing, timeline, next step. Tone: confident but human.
Set up an instant response message for a new lead enquiry. The reply should: acknowledge them by name, confirm we've received their enquiry, set a clear expectation for when they'll hear from a real person, and feel warm not robotic. Keep it under 80 words.
5
Set it up — optional but advised
Important — read this first
What we're building here is automation, not AI — and that distinction matters in sales more than anywhere else. No tool closes a deal better than a human. The goal is simple: use automation to handle the mechanics — instant acknowledgement, timed follow-ups, staying visible — so that by the time you pick up the phone or send that personal message, the lead is warm and expecting you. You do the selling. The workflow does the legwork.

AI conversation assistants, voice agents, and deeper sales automation are covered inside AI for Business Domination. What's below gets you started.
Log in to GoHighLevel (or sign up at gohighlevel.com) and create a new Workflow
Set the trigger: "Contact submitted a form" (or your landing page form source) — this fires the workflow the moment a lead signs up
Add a first action: send an instant confirmation email or SMS — use Claude to write it (warm, personal-feeling, sets expectation for human follow-up)
Add timed follow-up steps at Day 3 and Day 7 — these keep you visible while you prepare to have a real conversation. Then you take over.

Ready-to-use prompts
Here's a process I do regularly: [describe step by step]. Turn this into a clean, structured Standard Operating Procedure document anyone on my team could follow without asking me questions. Include why each step matters.
Here's my sales/operations data from the last month [paste]. Tell me: what's performing, what isn't, what stands out, and what action you'd recommend based on the patterns. Be direct — no fluff.
